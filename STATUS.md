# Bean Counter status

Bean Counter's current public product is the **local SQLite billing CLI**. The [v0.3.0 release](https://github.com/stevekkall-beansgc/bean-counter/releases/tag/v0.3.0) adds guided terminal setup and provides unsigned native packages tested on macOS 26.6.2 with Apple silicon and Ubuntu 24.04.5 x86-64/glibc 2.39. The packages include checksums, build provenance and dependency inventories. Other OS versions and Linux distributions, clean-account launch, signing and production host certification have not been verified; Windows is deferred.

The v0.4.2 release published the compatibility policy. The v0.4.3 documentation release added the owner-approved [billing roadmap](docs/billing-roadmap.md) and [M1 qualification record](docs/m1-current-format-qualification.md). The v0.5.0 release implements M2 customers and agreements and completes M2 for its qualified source-built local profile. M0 and M1 are complete; M3 is next and has not started. See the [canonical billing roadmap](docs/billing-roadmap.md), [M1 qualification](docs/m1-current-format-qualification.md), and [M2 migration qualification](docs/m2-migration-qualification.md) for exact support boundaries. The v0.3.0 release remains the latest native-package release; native artifacts are not qualified for M2.

The reduced local SQLite Phase 4 and the finance CSV Phase 5 are complete. The amended local Phase 6 is complete **with owner acceptance**. The owner waived its separate independent-review requirement; no independent technical review was completed or passed for this local release. Author validation and the owner's earlier synthetic v0.2.0 walkthrough are distinct from independent review. This status does not certify the original full platform plan.

The supported profile is one operator-controlled business per private installation, with multiple explicit customers and immutable customer agreements. The guided setup asks for actual terms and evidence without hand-authored JSON; unattended products retain the strict JSON interface. It retains fixed-price work, authorized outcomes and corrections, durable receipts, complete statements and reconciled finance CSV exports. It does not obtain customer consent, collect payment or guarantee that a downstream finance system ingests an export. Users provide and protect the machine, storage, backups and recovery process. The hard admission ceilings include 1,000 accepted decisions, 1,000 aliases, 1,000 permission changes, 32 MiB of economic payloads and a separate 32 MiB of alias ingress; these are limits, not capacity reservations or completion guarantees.

PostgreSQL product support, multi-host operation, remote authentication, hosting, automatic payments, resource guarantees and advanced comparison remain deferred. Historical PostgreSQL tests and design documents do not establish support for this billing profile. See [current requirements](CURRENT-REQUIREMENTS.md), the [billing quickstart](docs/billing-quickstart.md), [finance walkthrough](docs/finance-e2e.md) and [release profile](release/local-sqlite.md) for exact behavior and limitations.
