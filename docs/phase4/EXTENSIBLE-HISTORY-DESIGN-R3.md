## 2. Guarantees and trust boundary

Within the trusted authentication, authority and durable-storage boundary: no acknowledged first receipt is erased, replaced or made unfunded by optional work; no rollover resets identity, entitlement or gross expenditure; no partial closure, transfer effect or supplier release becomes authoritative. Unknown commit/durability remains unknown until resolved at the authoritative boundary.

Progress assumes eventual access to the authoritative journals, usable retained evidence, valid required authority and functioning storage. Revocation can suspend close; an unreachable fenced gateway can suspend drain; a lost authoritative journal can prevent recovery. None is DENY, expiry, permission to lose accepted work, or permission to release a supplier hold. A reserved receipt guarantees a funded import/disposition path under those assumptions, not ALLOW or unlimited later evidence/decision attempts.

Logical reservation enforcement is mandatory. Physical disk, filesystem and database durability remain real deployment assumptions. A host must account for its retained bytes and required working capacity and prevent competing work from spending protected quota. This does not assert that generic SQLite, PostgreSQL, disks or WAL can never fail. An unexpected I/O failure yields an operational failure/unknown outcome and recovery obligation, never an economic rejection. A deployment incapable of enforcing the logical resource contract cannot advertise this durable-ingress profile.

No mandatory cloud, hardware vendor, broker, model service or company-operated runtime is introduced. Users supply durable storage, backup/recovery and appropriate exclusive-writer mechanisms. Retention and work storage have real costs.

## 3. Bounded immutable segments and retained objects

Keep strict bounded UTF-8/JSON parsing, canonical UTF-16 object-key order, exact full tuple keys, decimal integers, no normalization, and domain-separated hashes. Atoms and existing commercial caps retain their current bounds. Persisted sequence/count encodings have maximum `M = 10^30 - 1`; §6 reserves representable headroom before acknowledgment or optional append. No counter may wrap, saturate silently, or acquire a longer encoding.

An execution segment contains exactly one complete atomic operation, its bounded exact companions, and its newly introduced trusted objects. Proposed ceilings remain 8 MiB resolved local segment bytes, 2 MiB newly introduced trusted bytes, and a 256 KiB operation envelope. Exact per-kind constants and escaping/framing proofs belong to the canonical candidate. No multi-operation partly filled tail exists in this first extensible profile. Packing is not necessary for correctness.

Header: profile, original enrollment/registration, segment ordinal, previous segment ID/content hash and previous replay root. Footer: exact bounded local object inventory with byte sizes, operation/result and resulting replay root. The next header points backward to the finalized footer. The segment and all authoritative head/index/economic changes commit atomically. A finalized segment never reopens. Staged bytes are not history without committed membership.

Prior immutable documents are referenced, not recopied into a lifetime catalog. Every referenced exact body must remain available and hash-verified; hashes alone do not establish assent, evidence or state. Per-case evidence remains bounded by 16 documents with 4 KiB preimages. Each operation has a closed bounded dependency list. A reader charges all fetched bytes to its explicit work budget, including old dependencies.

Large history, inventories and indexes use bounded pages and streaming cursors. Per-advance bytes/records/memory are bounded; total replay work grows with history. Budget exhaustion or cancellation returns INCOMPLETE with the exact selected prefix/cursor, never a successful truncated report. No operation embeds an unbounded list of cases or receipts. §6 specifies bounded index growth; §7 avoids fresh historical inventory copies at every close.

## 4. Permanent identities and proof of reconstruction completeness

Enrollment has one externally verified original base and one registration. Replay starts at that anchor and reconstructs every exact operation, companion, observation, result and mutable-state transition. Gaps, splices, repeated segments, changed bodies and rehashed invalid transitions fail. Store/index checkpoints accelerate a previously locally verified prefix; imported or self-asserted roots do not authorize omitted history. Fresh independent reconstruction must be possible without trusting such an accelerator.

Full delivery/control keys remain `[scope, source, external_id]` across old/new writers. Full case identity remains the full family key plus immutable event source/business ID. Registry occupancy, all aliases, exact submissions, first receipts, canceled identities and final cases are permanent. Check occupied delivery/control identity before semantic alias lookup. Rollover cannot recycle keys. Hashes index complete canonical tuples; no concatenated-string or family-name-only identity.

Reconstruction has an explicit required input `ExpectedPrefix` containing logical store identity, scope/target, profile, enrollment hash, terminal segment ordinal/ID and replay root. Its authority comes from a trusted primary snapshot/read observation or another explicitly trusted external head pin, **not** from the submitted chain or a caller choosing its own head. Replay succeeds as COMPLETE_TO_EXPECTED_PREFIX only when it reaches that exact head and verifies all preceding membership and required objects. Removing a valid suffix then fails head/ordinal equality. A deliberately selected historical prefix is allowed, but is labeled historical and bound to its authenticated selection.

Without an authoritative expected head, the strongest result is VERIFIED_SUPPLIED_PREFIX. It proves that prefix's internal validity, not completeness or latest state. If an authoritative head was obtained at time T, completeness is as of that pinned snapshot, not a claim about commits after T. The observation itself and how it was trusted appear in provenance.

Terminal history cannot be distinguished from an older valid prefix by a backward chain alone. A checkpoint's signature/hash cannot replace the authoritative head comparison. Comparison and retry responses carry the same distinction (§9 and §12).

## 5. Gateway writer authority, first receipt and approved offline intake

### 5.1 The designated durable boundary

A durable acknowledgment follows the atomic journal commit of exact request/evidence, first receipt, permanent local identity mapping and token binding. A transport arrival before that boundary is not a durable receipt. First receipt time and contiguous receipt position never change on retry/import. Gateway receipt positions and control-log positions are separate bounded counters; a control marker is not an invented business receipt.

Each registration has a permanent case-owner function over the full case tuple and up to four logical gateway identities. The same owner serializes all aliases and changed-submission checks for a case. Wrong-owner traffic is forwarded if possible or refused before durability. It cannot receive a competing first receipt. Source claims, including backfill, convey no commercial authority.

### 5.2 Actual single-writer fencing

Each logical gateway has **one authoritative durable journal**, not a set of independently writable copied journals. Its receipt-commit capability atomically checks the current writer epoch, active seal/round state, identity occupancy and token state in that journal. A process-local mutex, a process ID, a central expiring lease invisible to an offline writer, or central rejection of later uploads is insufficient.

A restart against the same authoritative journal first acquires its exclusive writer capability and recovers committed state. Read-only resolution/retry of an already committed transition does not append a new recovery record or increment an epoch. Repeated crashes can resume the same pending transition. A second live process cannot obtain a receipt-commit capability concurrently, even if it knows the signing key.

Replacement follows: (1) pre-fund its optional bounded handover records/workspace; (2) fence the old writer at the **actual receipt-commit boundary**; (3) establish its final authoritative journal head and recover all committed receipts, token bindings, returned-token tombstones, active rounds and outcome records through that head; (4) atomically install a strictly greater writer epoch and the same logical identities; (5) only then enable new receipt commits. With shared authoritative storage, an exclusive lock and transactional epoch can implement the boundary. With separate hardware, a trusted storage/power/commit-authority fence must make old receipt commits impossible before the new copy is enabled. No vendor-specific mechanism is required, but the capability must be real and tested.

A backup predating an offline receipt is insufficient even after the old machine is stopped. If complete authoritative state cannot be recovered, replacement intake remains blocked. A disconnected old writer cannot be declared fenced just by changing central metadata. Finite epoch headroom and handover costs are reserved before handover; unbounded replacements are not part of a finite close allocation.

An old receipt committed before the fence remains valid and can be reacknowledged; an old-epoch new commit cannot succeed. Receipt proofs retain their historical writer epoch and exact journal membership. New writers inherit old token identities through the recovered journal, never through reassignment to fresh receipt identities.

### 5.3 Gateway-local pre-grants precede every issued token

The selected semantic interfaces are `CreateLocalCapacityGrant(owner, envelope) -> LocalGrant`, `RegisterGrant(authenticated_grant)`, `ReserveIngress(registered_grant_id, namespace_binding) -> IssuedToken`, `ActivateToken(token)`, `CommitFirstReceipt(token, exact_submission)`, `ResolveGrantOrToken(identity)`, and terminal reconciliation. They are not a frozen wire ABI. A grant is operational storage authority, not commercial assent or ALLOW. Owner-selected B still permits the actual request to be unknown until the gateway later consumes the token.

**Hard issuance guard:** before a central allocation becomes a durable issued token, acquires an allocation ordinal, or enters any seal cutoff, it must atomically consume one unique authenticated grant slot whose complete worst-case gateway-local token envelope is **already durably reserved**. No center-only issued state exists. Center capacity without an eligible local grant is insufficient. Refusal creates no token, allocation ordinal, cutoff member or new gateway disposal obligation. A separately funded diagnostic refusal, if recorded, is not token issuance.

A local grant identifies the authoritative logical central store, scope/registration, logical gateway, permanent namespace/routing profile, unique never-reused grant ID, fixed request-envelope/resource-template version, resource vector, and exact durable local grant record/head proof. It binds the intended central authority so another independent center cannot consume it. The local record is authenticated through the same trusted journal/writer boundary as receipts. Its capacity promise persists across writer replacement and may not expire while the central outcome is unresolved.

The local envelope covers the complete future token path: activation/binding, either new first receipt or alias, exact bounded request/evidence retention, unused/unactivated/never-delivered disposal tombstone, disposition/grant-terminal records, permanent full-key and grant/token index growth, all required local counter headroom, and bounded staging/journal/rollback/recovery work. Charge a conservative complete branch envelope, not just a receipt slot. In particular, a token that never activates already has room to be permanently prohibited. Grant creation/export and its own resolution/retirement records and work are additional explicitly funded costs, not overhead later borrowed from close resources.

The ordered durable protocol is:

1. **LOCAL_HELD.** Before exporting a grant, the exclusive gateway journal atomically reserves the full local token envelope plus the grant's own creation/control/retirement resources and representable counters, commits the unique grant record and installs its permanent held state. A crash or lost acknowledgment is resolved using that grant ID. The grant never permits a receipt by itself. A gateway lacking this capacity refuses grant creation before creating the promise.
2. **REGISTERED_UNCLAIMED at the center.** The center verifies the authenticated exact grant, owner/registration/store binding, resource-template sufficiency and unique identity, and funds its own bounded grant-registration/terminal-retirement records, index/counter path and recovery work before registering it. Registration is idempotent, not token issuance, and creates no token allocation ordinal or seal-cutoff member. If registration cannot be funded, no central grant/token obligation is created; the gateway's already funded grant remains held until a later resolved registration/retirement. A lost registration response does not allow local reuse.
3. **CLAIMED / TOKEN_ISSUED.** Under central grant, capacity and allocation locks, reserve the full central token import/disposition/index/counter envelope, atomically mark that grant irreversibly claimed by one exact token, and commit that token's contiguous allocation ordinal and exact grant reference. No token may appear in the issued ledger or seal cutoff unless that atomic claim exists. One grant slot funds one token for its entire lifetime. Retries after an unknown commit resolve the existing grant-to-token mapping; they cannot choose another token or share the grant. Request bytes can still be unknown here.
4. **LOCAL_ACTIVATED.** On authenticated delivery of the issued-token proof, the gateway verifies the exact grant-to-token mapping and journal state, then converts the **existing** held envelope into that token's local backing and binding record. It does not first allocate disposal capacity at activation. A missing token delivery or activation acknowledgment leaves the same backing held. Local activation requires verified enrollment/capability and obeys the seal and writer guards. It confers no economic effect.
5. **TOKEN_TERMINAL / RECONCILED.** Local consumption or unused-return uses the already held envelope. During a seal, even a never-delivered token can be prohibited using its original LOCAL_HELD grant and the exact central claim proof, without a prior activation step or newly available local resources. The permanent terminal record binds both grant and token, so a delayed activation cannot revive either. Central import/disposition reconciliation consumes its reserved central envelope and records the same mapping. Actual retained bytes/identities remain charged. Only proven unused slack can return to the respective free resource accounts after exact terminal reconciliation; the grant ID and token ID never become reusable.

There is no distributed atomic transaction hidden in these steps. The safe intermediate state is excess held capacity. Unknown grant creation, registration, claim, delivery, activation or terminal outcome holds both sides' applicable reservations. Timeout, lease expiry, missing acknowledgment, a seal, an abort, central absence at an earlier prefix or non-observation cannot release or reassign a grant slot. The gateway never unilaterally withdraws an exported grant that the center might still claim.

An unclaimed exported grant may be retired only through a separate idempotent central `RETIRED_UNCLAIMED` transition, serialized against token issuance under the same grant lock and permanently forbidding later claim. The gateway releases proven unused local envelope only after verifying that terminal proof and recording its own permanent retirement. A grant not yet registered must first be registered (with its already local-held proof and newly funded central administrative path) and retired, rather than relying on an absence response that can race delayed registration. If the center cannot currently fund that administrative path, the local grant remains safely held; it is not an issued-token obligation and cannot enter a seal cutoff. All retirement costs were reserved at the side's grant-creation/registration transition. A claimed grant can retire only through its token's terminal protocol; a token-return message cannot masquerade as unclaimed retirement.

Gateway-local **control lifecycle backing** remains independently established before durable receipt capability: each registered gateway durably reserves its bounded shares of protected successful-close bundles and recovery workspace, and enrollment/capability verification retains their authenticated allocation references. Central quota alone cannot reserve remote gateway disk. CANCELLABLE rounds likewise secure both central and affected gateway-local optional backing before BEGIN. These bounded close/control bundles never pay for an arbitrary number of token tombstones; each such path was prepaid by that token's unique grant before central issuance.

One token is consumed at most once. Consuming it and creating a new case's first receipt (or its explicit alias binding) is one local transaction; central import later converts its reserved central resources to actual retained work without a gap. Known aliases use an explicitly reserved alias operation and the original first receipt; they do not designate another receipt. A changed payload at an occupied key conflicts permanently. Authentic but economically ineligible claims remain importable and retainable; a commercial DENY is a separate authorized decision.

### 5.4 Permanent delivery namespaces and full-case owner routing (selected B)

`namespace_binding = OwnedNamespace(namespace_descriptor, owner, case_route_version)` is reserved centrally before disconnection. The immutable descriptor denotes a disjoint subset of full delivery tuples, using an unambiguous bounded namespace encoding. It retains the original full tuple, not a rewritten substitute. Namespace creation proves no conflicting old occupancy and reserves the entire subset against **all** legacy/new writers, including unused keys. A centrally held namespace owner record precedes token issuance; absence of an individual imported key is not permission for another writer to use it.

The supplied key must belong to this gateway's namespace and its full semantic case must select the same permanent owner. Once local occupied-key lookup has rejected any changed payload, that owner may bind one unused token to a previously unknown bounded request and first receipt atomically. Full worst-case import, evidence/index growth and terminal/drain resources were reserved before this binding. It keeps local key/case uniqueness, first receipt and token binding permanently. Tokens cannot move between disconnected owners. Cases that do not match the delivery namespace are refused before durability, not silently relabeled or assigned elsewhere.

Proposed closed namespace encoding: a descriptor `(scope, owner_tag, logical_gateway_id)` reserves every full delivery tuple in that scope whose external ID begins with the exact ASCII prefix `gw1.<owner_tag>.`. `owner_tag` is 32 lowercase hexadecimal characters, unique and permanently registered in the scope; the nonempty remaining external-ID suffix occupies at most 91 UTF-8 bytes, keeping the complete ID within 128 bytes. The source component remains exact and independently authenticated; the prefix reservation applies across source values in that scope. The descriptor does not delegate commercial/source permissions. No normalization, prefix stripping or replacement ID is allowed. Existing raw IDs outside an owned prefix retain their old meaning. A tag/prefix containing any prior incompatible occupancy cannot be registered; reserve a genuinely unused one rather than reinterpreting old keys. Additive index/namespace enforcement must establish that absence under the shared scope lock before activation, then protect the entire range against every writer. Tags and namespace ownership are permanent even after closure. Encoding is an engineering proposal requiring independent canonical review, not an additional owner choice.

Enrollment pins the complete ordered logical-gateway list and routing version. The route uses a fixed profile-domain hash over the **canonical complete case tuple** (full family key, exact event source and business-event ID), interpreted as an unsigned integer modulo the list length (one through four); it selects the indexed logical gateway. Hashing here partitions work; identity still compares full canonical tuples. A change in endpoint or writer epoch does not change this list, namespace owner or case route. A case's delivery aliases must stay in the selected owner's permanent namespace. Reassignment/rerouting is outside this profile.

The admission order is precise: bounded authenticated parsing; determine delivery-key ownership without doing a case alias lookup; if this gateway is not authoritative for that key, return an operational wrong-owner/unavailable response before durability, or forward the exact bytes to the owner. Do not assert that the key is free. At the authoritative owner, inspect permanent full delivery occupancy first: changed bytes conflict; exact bytes return the original receipt/status knowledge. Only for a free key compute/check full-case routing, then inspect the permanent case/submission identity. A route/namespace mismatch refuses before durability. It cannot be rescued by aliasing some other case, assigning another owner, changing the supplied ID or selecting a later receipt. Thus cross-owner operational routing never bypasses occupied-key precedence at the key's authority.

For a free delivery key and a new case, one unused preissued token is atomically bound locally to exact request/evidence, full key/case and the one first receipt. For a free key aliasing an identical existing submission, a token funds only the new permanent alias mapping and later central import; its immutable disposition identifies ALIAS and the original designated receipt/token, with no new first-receipt position/time. Reserving worst-case new-case cost for each unbound token is conservative; slack returns only after durable central reconciliation. Central import can import the original token before an earlier-issued alias token without advancing the allocation reconciliation cursor out of order. A cursor is a completeness check, not an import-order constraint; this prevents an alias dependency from deadlocking a drain.

A supplied arbitrary legacy key outside the preowned namespace still needs an authoritative online path. The approved offline interface accepts brand-new requests with valid owner-consistent keys, not arbitrary colliding legacy IDs at arbitrary disconnected gateways. Option A's per-request prebinding prerequisite is not selected. Each token is issued before the unknown request exists; the eventual binding is made at the exclusive gateway's durable transaction. Registration pins these B rules; no silent namespace/routing switch is permitted during an outage or after conflict.

## 6. Resource accounting, counters and bounded index growth

### 6.1 Accounts and resource classes

For each retained-resource dimension d at every committed prefix:

`used[d] + held_grants[d] + held_ingress[d] + held_close[d] + held_optional[d] <= provisioned[d]`.

At the gateway, `held_grants` contains each not-yet-converted local envelope and its remaining grant-control costs; token activation moves backing to `held_ingress` without an uncharged gap or double pledge. Grant-specific terminal costs remain held until used or terminally reconciled. At the center, registered unclaimed grants hold their own funded administrative/retirement envelope; token issuance separately funds central ingress while consuming the unique local-grant claim. Grant/token maps are permanent. Receipt/control counter headroom and index growth are held at the same transitions as bytes.

Resources are owned by explicit immutable allocation IDs; they are not promises based on a free-space guess. Include canonical/data/trust bytes, object/record and permanent-index entries, all bounded observations and proof records, gateway-local records, and every relevant sequence value. Temporary workspace has a separate reusable peak-capacity account; its held credits cannot also back retained bytes. Money/funding and storage capacity are separate accounts with different authority.

The host enforces quota across all admitted writers and work consumers. A bounded mandatory-progress workspace lane is protected at enrollment/activation; optional writers/readers cannot monopolize it. Work is serialized into that lane when necessary. A comparison snapshot or other reader that pins database/WAL/version retention must be charged to its own bounded work budget, canceled/released before it can exhaust the protected lane, or rejected by the backend capability gate. This is a requirement for the real-store implementation, not a claim that a generic pool setting already provides it.

### 6.2 Explicit owners of costs

| Allocation | Retained cost owner | Reusable work cost owner |
|---|---|---|
| Local grant and central grant registration | Before local export: gateway grant creation, permanent identity/index/counter path, complete worst-case local token envelope including never-activated disposal, and grant retirement/reconciliation records. Before central registration: its bounded registration/retirement and claim-registry index/counter path. Unknown outcomes keep these credits held | Prepaid bounded local/central grant verification, staging, journal and recovery slots; retries reuse grant identity, not new grant records |
| Issued ingress token | Before central issuance: central token/import/disposition/permanent-index/counter envelope and atomic unique grant claim. Gateway-local activation, receipt-or-alias-or-unused-return, request/evidence, disposition/tombstone/index/counter costs already belong to its durable local grant; activation converts rather than creates them | Prepaid gateway grant/token slot plus the central mandatory lane for parsing/import/verification, database transaction/log/checkpoint workspace |
| Protected successful-close bundle | One finish-only round's central begin, at most four gateway seal markers, four drain-complete certificates, bounded READY marker, final atomic closure/release segment, and per-gateway terminal-outcome installation; all their counters/heads | Bounded central/gateway seal verification and final transaction workspace |
| Optional cancellable-round bundle | Every round-specific success **and** abort alternative charged conservatively before begin, including begin, markers, READY, terminal decision, delayed-outcome handling records if any and terminal gateway installations; it never borrows protected close credits | Its complete peak workspace or an explicitly acquired share of the protected mandatory lane that cannot starve finishing work |
| Optional replacement/other operation | Its exact bounded records, permanent identities and counter/index growth; no borrowing from outstanding ingress/close | Its own bounded workspace admission |

Grant IDs and their claim/retirement records are permanent and cannot be recycled after slack is released. Grant creation/registration and token issuance have separate paid lifecycle records, but allocation/account updates inside a transition do not recursively create an unfunded operation. Token import/disposition outputs are permanent global progress and reused after an aborted round. A round does not pay to recopy them. A previously terminal token consumes no new terminal record when inspected by another round. Allocation/account updates and their immutable provenance are included in the operation templates above, not an unbudgeted recursive extra operation.

Temporary costs include parser buffers, page verification, bounded staging, affected-index page changes, database journal/WAL overhead and rollback/recovery working space. Their backend-specific peaks need a conservative measured/proved envelope and concurrency limit. Repeated crashes may cause unbounded cumulative I/O, but reuse a bounded number of staging slots keyed by allocation/transition ID. They cannot accumulate a fresh orphan artifact for every retry. Unknown membership holds the slot until read-only authoritative resolution; it is not blindly overwritten. Aborted transaction work is reclaimed/recovered before admitting another attempt into that slot. Actual storage failure outside the guaranteed envelope remains a durability incident.

### 6.3 Immutable inventory pages reused across rounds

An allocation ordinal is contiguous per gateway/registration. Each token eventually has exactly one immutable disposition: CONSUMED (with NEW_CASE receipt binding or ALIAS binding to the existing original receipt), or RETURNED_UNUSED with a terminal local tombstone. Each disposition binds the exact unique grant-to-token claim; ALIAS never adds a receipt position. Dispositions and receipt log leaves use a persistent bounded-page authenticated index. Updates create only the bounded changed path; old roots remain valid. Leaves are never recopied en masse for a new round. Local grant creation reserves its worst-case local token/grant disposition and index pages; central registration funds its own grant path, and central token issuance reserves its worst-case token reconciliation/index pages. A not-yet-activated token therefore has its local disposition-page capacity before appearing in the allocation ledger.

The center has a monotonic verified allocation-prefix cursor per gateway and separate imported receipt-prefix evidence. It advances only after the next exact allocation's terminal disposition and, if consumed, the exact central import have been committed and checked. Each cursor advance is paid by that token at most once. Out-of-order imported receipts may wait; they do not falsely advance over a gap. A crash before an advance resumes it under the same token identity. A crash after it returns its saved result. A round stores only its fixed-size cutoff/root/count references and terminal verification marker. Rechecking an already reconciled prefix reads existing pages/roots and writes no per-token copy or audit entry.

A verifier without a trusted local cursor rebuilds it by reading existing issuance/disposition/import records. Replay may take O(history) reads, but neither this verification nor another close creates O(history) new retained pages. Optional diagnostic access logs cannot be charged to the mandatory close guarantee.

### 6.4 Representable counters are capacity

For each finite counter c, maintain checked integers `q_c` (highest consumed value) and `R_c` (maximum additional increments needed to finish all admitted mandatory obligations). At every proposed commit:

`0 <= q_c <= M` and `0 <= R_c <= M - q_c`.

Reserve local-grant creation/terminal/token increments before local grant export, central grant-registration/retirement increments before registration, and central token/round/template increments **before** creating their respective durable obligations, including future central segment ordinals, gateway receipt/control positions, allocation/round ordinals where needed, head revisions, imported/terminal/prefix counts and bounded index cardinalities. An already-issued allocation ordinal is used, not reserved twice. A future mandatory gateway receipt consumes its previously held receipt-position credit. Optional work may advance c only if the post-state inequality still holds. One decrement of R funds at most the corresponding bounded actual increment(s); uncertainty releases no headroom. All arithmetic checks before addition/subtraction/multiplication; no machine-width overflow can precede the validation.

The worksheet is a multiset of all remaining **distinct one-time** transition slots, including whichever mutually exclusive branch has the largest cost or their conservative sum. Idempotent retries and read-only recovery do not consume a fresh slot. New attempts have new bundles. No-progress retries therefore cannot take the last representable import or close ordinal, even when disk bytes remain.

Permanent resources do not become free just because an operation/case becomes final. Optional resource extension adds backing with its own reserved operation and counter costs; it cannot extend the fixed numeric encoding after exhaustion. Refusal before creating the next promise is honest finite backpressure, not an economic expiry.

### 6.5 A bounded logical index design

Use bounded canonical full-key encodings and a fixed-depth radix/prefix tree for resource accounting, rather than asserting constant splits in a tree whose height grows unchecked. The candidate defines maximum encoded key length K (including domain tags, lengths, full tuple components and bounded ordinals), fixed fanout and maximum page bytes P. The logical maximum path length L is derived from K, not current history size. Empty/terminal key markers are unambiguous. Separate immutable value bodies are bounded and referenced; node entries cannot contain unbounded collision buckets or version lists. Full keys, not a truncated hash, determine uniqueness. Derived index pages are charged backend storage/work resources, not extra commercial companions or trusted facts. Their roots cannot replace canonical replay; any authoritative new body introduced by an operation still belongs to its bounded exact segment inventory.

Reserve conservatively up to L+1 new/changed logical path pages per inserted/updated index, plus its bounded value pages and root update, for every operation template. Persistent old nodes remain charged as retained history if referenced. Physical SQL indexes/storage layouts may differ, but each backend must supply a conservative bounded-depth/address-space and page/WAL growth envelope or enforce one explicitly; arbitrary average-case B-tree cost is not a valid reservation proof. No numeric K/P/template table is fabricated here: canonical authoring must derive it from the closed schemas and both-store implementation must validate its physical envelope.

Before enrollment, local grant export, central grant registration and token issuance, every applicable obligatory operation template must fit the actual per-operation canonical limits and all provisioned backing/workspace bounds for the configured keys, gateway count and maximum request envelope. An oversized template makes that configuration ineligible for acknowledgment; it is not permission to exempt import/closure from limits. Index growth may not be concealed inside uncharged dependencies. Readers separately account for derived page traversal without treating the whole transitive archive as one bounded segment.

## 7. Pre-funded resumable seal/drain/close state machine

### 7.1 Choosing the round and its budget before BEGIN

Only one active close round exists per registration. Its permanent identity is `(registration, round_ordinal, exact_command_hash)`, with immutable requested family set, mode and allocation references. A batch has a designated original-family bundle owner; other family bundles remain untouched until final release. Enrollment reserves up to 32 successful-close bundles, each large enough for the bounded family/gateway topology. An unused bundle for a newly closed family can be released only after that close and its gateway terminal-outcome installation obligations remain fully funded.

Two modes make cancellation costs explicit:

- **FINISH_ONLY:** may use a protected successful-close bundle. Once BEGIN commits it can pause/recover/resume under the same identity, but cannot abort. Caller cancellation stops waiting; it does not cancel the durable round or free its resources. Requests to abort are operationally refused. Authority loss suspends final closure rather than bypassing current authority. The finite finish path remains reserved.
- **CANCELLABLE:** must acquire a separate complete optional bundle before BEGIN. That bundle funds all round-specific success and abort paths, gateway outcomes, pages/observations/counter values and recovery workspace. It does not draw on the protected successful-close bundles. An abort preserves those bundles for a later round. The mode cannot be upgraded after irreversible work by assuming later funding will appear.

Both modes use already-owned token import/disposition credits for token-specific work; those outputs are reusable irrespective of the round result. If no optional capacity exists, the system may still begin a FINISH_ONLY round from the protected bundle. It may not promise cancellability. This closes the saturated-prefix hole without permitting a retrospective unfunded abort.

### 7.2 Central state machine

| Durable state | Transition and guard | Retry/recovery behavior |
|---|---|---|
| No active round | BEGIN atomically pins identity, mode, entire funding, prior terminal round, finite per-gateway allocation cutoffs containing only atomically issued, uniquely grant-backed tokens; freezes new ordinary permit issuance for this registration | Unknown BEGIN resolves same identity. Never mint a substitute round on timeout |
| DRAINING | Request exact-round seals from fenced gateways; verify token dispositions/imports and monotonic prefix cursors; retain each bounded seal/completion once | Already committed token and round transitions return saved results. Missing progress resumes in place |
| READY | All fenced cutoffs fully reconciled and seals installed; bounded readiness marker binds exact verified heads | Revalidation may read current authority/family/economic heads; failed validation appends no new observation |
| COMMITTED | One transaction checks current authority and all observed heads, closes requested families, installs set transfer and mandatory last-supplier release, appends terminal result | Unknown outcome resolves same command. No abort may race past this terminal state |
| ABORTED | Only CANCELLABLE, before COMMITTED; atomic terminal CAS from DRAINING/READY, funded by its original bundle; no economic closure/release | Retained token progress survives. Replay returns same terminal abort |

Gateway terminal outcome delivery follows COMMITTED/ABORTED. The center retains the bounded delivery/install obligation until each fenced gateway acknowledges its exact terminal installation; no new round begins while a prior required installation is unresolved. Repeated sends and reads are idempotent, not new immutable attempts. A gateway may be unavailable indefinitely, but its pending outcome remains funded. Terminal installation after COMMITTED must precede releasing the remainder of that round's resources.

Optional updates during DRAINING/READY cannot spend held resources or close families through another route. Final authority/head validation is current under locks. New invalid/stale observations are not appended speculatively on each validation attempt. Exactly the successful committed observation belongs in the final segment. A long pause does not require another round, refreshed receipt or new clock record merely to resume.

### 7.3 Gateway state machine and delayed messages

Gateway control state includes logical identity, writer epoch, last installed terminal round/outcome, and zero or one active exact round ID. States are OPEN -> SEALING(R) -> SEALED(R) -> OPEN-after-terminal(R). All receipt commits check this state inside the authoritative journal transaction.

SEAL_BEGIN(R) verifies the authenticated central BEGIN, its exact registration, cutoff, predecessor terminal round, mode/funding commitment and local backing. It serializes after all earlier receipt commits, then forbids new first receipts and token consumption for this registration. For every unresolved issued token through the cutoff, the center supplies its authenticated atomic claim and exact durable-local-grant reference. The gateway verifies the unique claim against its retained LOCAL_HELD/activated/terminal grant state and uses that token's **pre-issuance local envelope**, not spare disk, activation-time allocation or the fixed close-metadata reserve. A consumed token retains its exact pre-seal receipt; an unused/unactivated/never-delivered token gets its prepaid durable grant-and-token tombstone preventing later activation/consumption. Not-yet-delivered allocated tokens cannot be omitted. An issued token lacking a valid unique pre-backed grant is an invalid protocol state, not an accepted obligation repaired by borrowing close capacity. Unclaimed grants are not issued tokens and do not enter the token cutoff or require token tombstones merely because a seal occurs; their backing remains held unless separately terminally retired. The seal itself releases no grant. SEALED(R) binds the exact cutoff, immutable token-disposition root/count and actual receipt high-water/root. Nothing after this marker can become an earlier receipt.

Exact duplicate BEGIN, page, seal or outcome messages reuse their existing state/result. A lower/terminal round's message cannot alter the active round. A future BEGIN cannot skip the installed predecessor terminal result. Every RESUME/ABORT/COMMIT message names the exact active round and authenticated central terminal outcome; a delayed ABORT(R) cannot unseal R+1. A token's immutable disposition may still be imported after its reporting round ended, because it is a token fact, but it cannot alter another round's control state.

CANCELLABLE abort may arrive before a gateway saw BEGIN. The gateway installs a bounded terminal tombstone for that exact round/predecessor and refuses any delayed BEGIN for it. If it is already SEALING/SEALED(R), it atomically installs the same terminal outcome and resumes without erasing already written dispositions/receipts. It need not finish unused-token traversal to abort; unprocessed tokens remain activated/reserved as before and can be used after resume. Already returned tokens remain unusable. A partially prepared inventory root is never represented as a complete seal. All control-path variants were funded at BEGIN.

Outcome installation after COMMITTED also records the genuine central closure-time lower bound. Before another receipt, the trusted gateway clock must reach that bound. Do not clamp, backdate or manufacture timestamps. Equal-clock receipt order uses the retained serialized order. If the clock is behind, keep new intake blocked. An abort introduces no fictitious close time; it preserves any earlier real closure lower bound.

### 7.4 Drain verification and repeated rounds

The center verifies actual bounded token leaves/bodies and unique authenticated local-grant claims against its exact issued allocation set through each cutoff. Allocation membership requires the atomic grant claim, including for never-delivered tokens; center-only reservations are not valid issued members. For every token: exactly one terminal disposition; each consumed receipt exact and centrally imported; each returned token permanently prohibited at the gateway. Receipt positions through the seal must be contiguous, unique and imported. Completeness is established by the monotonic verified prefixes plus the exact seal roots/counts, not caller-provided hashes alone.

Each newly reconciled token advances the global cursor once, with its own paid immutable evidence. Already reconciled prefixes are reused in subsequent rounds even if a former round aborted. A new round adds at most bounded round-specific metadata; it does not recopy a lifetime inventory or rewrite each old token with a new round ID. Partial page verification uses the fixed work slot, commits only bounded valid progress, and resumes from committed cursor. A crash does not reset a verified cursor or require a new seal generation.

Unfenced gateways do not block close. Their issued/consumed-unknown tokens stay charged and retain an import path. Later import enters adjustment with actual earlier/equal/later receipt chronology; permit issuance time is not receipt time. A close's completeness claim explicitly excludes unobserved unfenced receipts (§12). Fenced or unfenced, no token may be abandoned because a supplier hold has been released.

### 7.5 Why saturated-prefix progress remains funded

For a valid FINISH_ONLY round with a finite issued cutoff, each issued token already consumed one unique grant that durably held its complete local envelope **before central issuance**, and central issuance already reserved its central envelope. Thus every remaining token, including never-delivered or unactivated tokens, has a finite set of unspent disposition/import/reconciliation slots, local permanent index/counter headroom and recovery work; every gateway has at most its bounded seal and terminal-install transitions; the round has its remaining READY and final close transitions. Recovery performs read-only resolution or the next uncommitted one-time transition. Each successful transition consumes its own reserved resources and decreases this finite remaining-transition measure; retries add no retained transition. Grant-backed disposal requires no new local allocation, grant creation or activation prerequisite during the finish path. Lost messages may delay progress but cannot remove its backing. After token drain, bounded closure and terminal installations finish. This argument assumes eventual gateway/storage/authority availability, not a deadline or throughput bound.

For CANCELLABLE, abort uses its prefunded terminal branch and pending gateway installation slots. It consumes no protected successful-close bundle. It does not undo token progress or recreate token costs. Repeating new cancellable rounds requires new optional bundles and counter headroom. When those are unavailable, reject another cancellable BEGIN while preserving a FINISH_ONLY path. There is no sequence of accepted unfunded aborts that spends the last close capacity.

## 8. Atomic transfer, unresolved cases and economic continuity

A bounded closure certificate contains the newly explicitly closed families, complete newly unavailable ordinary-family set (at most 32), prerequisite/family heads, central visibility predecessor, verified gateway cutoff references, original terms/entitlement references and complete required supplier before/after transitions. It does not enumerate pending cases.

At a selected committed prefix: a case finalized before closure stays final; a then-pending ordinary case centrally admitted before an affecting certificate becomes adjustment-pending at that certificate; a later admission to an unavailable ordinary family starts on adjustment with its true delayed-import/post-closure/unresolved-clock reason. The first affecting certificate supplies immutable transfer lineage `(case_id, certificate_id)`. A later ALLOW/DENY changes current lifecycle but **does not erase its earlier transfer or historical pending state**. Replay applies events in order, rather than projecting today's final flag backward.

Every authoritative decision, retry lifecycle view and comparison evaluates this rule at its named prefix. Per-case materialized flags are caches, never independent authority. Thus more than 256 unresolved cases transfer atomically without an unbounded transaction. Bounded on-demand witnesses name exact admission, affecting certificate and prefix; they are derived views, not extra economic writes.

One family-state serialization boundary orders admission, ordinary award and closure. Award first consumes the entitlement and remains final; close first makes an ordinary command invalid. Do not silently reinterpret it as adjustment. Unavailable-prerequisite propagation traverses the bounded original graph, stops at actually accepted prerequisites, and remains distinct from explicit family closure.

The last explicit supplier-family close atomically releases every remaining held atom, including a zero-held transition, in the same final segment as the certificate and transfer effect. No later release-only repair exists. Earlier closes can retain capacity for remaining open supplier families. Transfer itself uses no adjustment money and promises no award.

Ordinary ALLOW retains original terms, occurrence/evidence requirements, current authority, original supplier capacity and prerequisite rules. Sender backfill remains an assertion. One permanent full-family entitlement spans all cases, segments and ordinary/positive/negative/zero adjustment paths; at most one ALLOW consumes it. Zero consumes entitlement without a zero posting. DENY settles only its case.

Signed adjustments retain independent assent/delegation, exact payer/bearer and directional recipient, current authority, direction and total gross caps and shared funding pool. Retain signed reference amount and positive directional transfer magnitude without applying the sign twice. +100 and -150 require gross 250. Negative/zero adjustments, supplier release and rollover replenish neither gross caps nor original supplier capacity. Adjustments remain first separate entitlements, not invented refunds, appeals or second awards.

## 9. Exact retries and truthful offline lifecycle responses

Receipt knowledge and lifecycle knowledge are separate fields. An exact retry can return the **identical original immutable receipt** without minting a new acknowledgment timestamp, importing again or consuming an additional history slot. Current read/access checks still apply. If the host cannot establish the required permission offline, it refuses disclosure operationally; this design does not waive authority because the receipt is cached.

The lifecycle view is one of:

- `AUTHORITATIVE_AT_PREFIX`: lifecycle reconstructed at a primary read's exact trusted prefix, with that head/observation. Current means current at that read snapshot only.
- `CACHED_VERIFIED_PREFIX`: last verified lifecycle and complete prefix reference; explicitly `current_lifecycle = UNKNOWN`. It is historical even if believed recent.
- `UNKNOWN`: no verified central lifecycle available; return no invented pending/ordinary/adjustment/final status.

A disconnected gateway uses the latter two forms. It may return its durable receipt while sealed or while central close/ALLOW/DENY may have committed unseen. It must not call its cached ordinary-pending flag current, infer central ABSENT from lack of a reply, or infer DENY from capacity refusal. A receipt not yet imported has `central_admission = UNKNOWN` unless an authoritative named-prefix observation proves absence at that prefix. Such absence never denies gateway durability.

An exact control retry returns its original saved result; any separately supplied lifecycle/status observation carries its own prefix and knowledge label. A new free delivery alias is a distinct capacity-funded mapping operation, not an unlimited free retry. Changed occupied-key bytes conflict before alias resolution. Offline responses do not create canonical lifecycle rows merely to record repeated transport attempts.

## 10. Transactions, recovery and store boundaries

One coordinator checks trust, economics and complete resource/identity rules; only it constructs validated plans. Concrete SQLite/PostgreSQL adapters assert observed heads and persist complete plans. They do not choose ALLOW/DENY or compute prices. Shared legacy/new identity and namespace enforcement cannot be bypassed by old-profile writers.

Integration must extend the actual existing global lock order once: enrollment/identity/namespace scope, capacity/allocation, gateway/round, family/prerequisite, case, entitlement, original supplier and adjustment pool heads in a consistent total order. Discover extra locks -> rollback -> restart in order. Gateway-local commit fencing has its own serial transaction; it is not falsely represented as distributed atomicity with the center. Tokens bridge that gap through durable outstanding obligations and eventual exact reconciliation.

Within each central transaction, segment membership/head, immutable records, identity/index updates, counter/reservation conversion, economic state and held intentions commit together. Failed/uncommitted plans change none of them. Unknown commit resolves the same identity on the authoritative store before reattempt, abort or cleanup. No public raw append bypass.

Restart preserves exact local-grant holds, registration/claim/retirement maps and allocation/activation/token/round state. Gateway replacement must recover unactivated/exported grants as well as activated tokens; omitting a held grant cannot enable replacement intake or make its capacity free. Already committed transitions are read and returned, not appended again. Bounded staging slots may be reclaimed only after authoritative non-membership and allocation resolution. A gateway first receipt is never an orphan merely because central admission is absent. Reopen means the same journal/database and identities, not reopened ordinary rights, a writable finalized segment or reset funding.

Successful replay requires access to exact retained objects; mutable accelerators are rebuilt when necessary. Archive pruning, migration/cutover, offline database copying and recovery that rolls back acknowledged history are outside this Phase 4 design. No Phase 5 export/restore implementation is implied by lifecycle projection requirements.

## 11. Authoritative prefix and gateway-coverage completeness

A complete central-prefix reconstruction is not automatically complete durable ingress. Every result claiming completeness carries: the trusted `ExpectedPrefix`; requested scope and whether historical/current-at-read; verification status; and one bounded coverage entry per registered gateway.

Each gateway coverage entry names its logical identity/mode, allocation cutoff and disposition root/count, receipt high-water/root, verified central reconciliation/import prefix, corresponding seal/observation or `UNKNOWN`, and outstanding/unknown allocation scope as known at the selected central prefix. Counts are checked and cannot imply that an unobserved token is unused. Coverage proofs themselves must be committed/referenced by that selected prefix or labeled external observations not included in its replay.

Allowed claims:

- `COMPLETE_CENTRAL_PREFIX(P)`: every committed segment/object through authoritative P was reconstructed. There may still be durable unreported gateway receipts.
- `COMPLETE_GATEWAY_CUTOFF(G,C,H)`: every issued allocation through C has a verified terminal disposition, and every consumed receipt through the exact sealed high-water H has been imported at P. This is a bounded explicit scope, not all receipts for all future time.
- `CENTRAL_KNOWN_ONLY` / `UNRECONCILED` / `UNKNOWN_GATEWAY_COVERAGE`: state the narrower knowledge; do not manufacture completeness.

To claim complete ingress **through a set of gateway cutoffs**, require valid coverage for every gateway in that declared set and sufficient central P containing the imports. To claim coverage for the whole registration through those cutoffs, include all registered gateways, including normally unfenced ones. Ordinary close need not wait for unfenced gateways, so it often cannot make that stronger registration-wide claim. It may legitimately report a complete central prefix with unknown unfenced coverage. No report asserts all offline receipts as of wall-clock now from a root or issuance count alone.

Terminal suffix truncation, a stale checkpoint and an omitted gateway are different failures/limitations and retain different labels. Coverage never authorizes an economic adjustment or modifies chronology.

## 12. Nonposting comparison

Comparison pins an authoritative central snapshot, reconstructs its entire selected chronology and publishes §11 coverage beside its result. It includes original base/terms, first receipts, admissions, supplements, rejected claims, authority/evidence observations, token/round facts relevant to chronology, closure effects and final economic companions. Unknown offline work is not invented or silently declared absent.

Historical original terms must reproduce every actual economic prefix. Prospective policy candidates run independently over the same observed chronology and actual discretionary outcomes; they cannot overwrite historical terms or invent counterfactual ALLOW/DENY. Actually retained authorized adjustments/corrections preserve their real terms/time and are distinct from simulations. Unsupported policy substitutions are reported unsupported. Broader changed-eligibility/discretion comparisons remain a separate product choice.

For a candidate that violates entitlement, supplier/funding/cap rules, report the first infeasible prefix without a comparable partial total. Reports are explicitly nonposting and scoped to the pinned central prefix and stated gateway coverage. A complete central-only report can be valid while some offline ingress is unknown; its conclusion must not say it covers that work. Later imports produce a later report.

Use SELECT-only snapshot interfaces, bounded computation and explicit cursors. No business locks, authoritative aliases, postings, intentions or capacity allocations arise from evaluation. Reader work quotas from §6 protect mandatory progress. No inherited 999/1,024 lifetime-step limit may truncate history into an apparent complete result. Expected arithmetic comes from an independent oracle, not production evaluator calls.
