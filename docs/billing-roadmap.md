## Product goal and boundary

Bean Counter is a self-hosted billing engine for one business per installation, with multiple customers in the 1.0 target. Applications use the language-neutral CLI/JSON interface. Operators provide and administer the host, storage and backups. The product records agreed charges and retained evidence; it does not determine whether work was valuable or establish customer consent on its own.

OSS 1.0 targets a practical billing lifecycle: fixed and quantity-based work, explicit outcomes and corrections, agreement changes, customer-facing billing periods, recurrence, approved late facts and immutable statements. Each customer selects a billing schedule from centrally governed supported terms. The business configures a separate fiscal reporting calendar for internal reports. No built-in Stripe or other payment-provider adapter, payment execution or provider refund flow is included. Multi-business tenancy and Bean-operated infrastructure are deferred until Bean Labs decides whether it wants to own infrastructure. Remote APIs, multi-host writers, payment-card handling, a general tax engine, tax/legal invoice claims, universal currencies and automatic collection are also outside the current target.

## Approved M2 rules

- The agreement and price for a work record are selected by the ledger timestamp of its first successful acceptance. Caller-reported work time remains separate evidence and does not backdate pricing. An exact retry returns the original result and price selection.
- Accepting a request for work does not create a charge by itself. A separately submitted billable-work record may create the charge specified by the active agreement.
- Starting, amending or changing the price creates an immutable agreement version with an explicit effective time. Changes do not reprice accepted work. An agreement end blocks new billable work after its effective time while preserving accepted history and exact-identity retry resolution.
- Customer-scoped commands require an explicit customer. Delivery and semantic retry identities are scoped by `(source, customer)`: different customers may reuse an application ID, identical same-scope retries return the original result, and conflicting reuse refuses. Wrong-customer or unauthorized operations refuse without disclosure or mutation.
- Operator-configured customer/source authority remains explicit. M2 adds no automatic expiry or deletion of accepted records or retry identities; changing that policy requires a separate owner decision.

## Owner-approved M4 and M5 policy directions (2026-09-27)

- Failed work is chargeable only when the applicable billing arrangement explicitly says so. There is no implicit failed-work charge. The M4 profile does not implement an explicit failed-work fee and refuses failed work before charge; explicit failed-work pricing is deferred beyond M4.
- M4 quantity is per work record, not a cumulative activity reading. Cumulative activity belongs to a separate billing-period model under M5, with any unit conversion at period end.
- The owner withdrew four-decimal rounding after identifying AI token-scale usage. The M4 quantity profile must preserve token-granularity charges: use exact rate-by-quantity arithmetic and book at USD scale 18, within the existing core limit, so a typical fractional-cent amount is not rounded to zero per work record. Preserve full booked precision in statements and exports; do not round each work record or statement total to four decimal places. The existing fixed-price profile and accepted USD scale-2 records remain unchanged. Any billing-cycle payable rounding and regulatory presentation customization remain M5 decisions.
- Use the existing operator-configured customer/source authority for usage assertions and retain the source assertion and receipt evidence. Keep the existing outcome authority and evidence terms. The service must check authority; a submitted assertion is not independent proof of work or consent.

These directions preserve every approved M2 rule above, including agreement selection at first successful ledger acceptance, the original terms and accepted history, and delivery and semantic retry scope under `(source, customer)`.

An absent outcome does not by itself cancel an existing initial charge. The M4 usage profile rates successful `content.generated` work by one explicitly configured unit and integer quantity per agreement, with a maximum quantity and a positive USD rate of at most 18 fractional digits. It rejects failed work before charge. The profile has no minimum charge, does not amend an accepted base usage quantity, and uses the existing authorized outcome and outcome-correction contract. Accepted base records remain immutable. Additional billable milestones, multiple units within one agreement, minimum charges, explicit failed-work pricing, usage-quantity corrections, and cycle-aware post-close adjustments are deferred beyond this M4 profile; cycle-bounded adjustment behavior belongs to M5.

### M5 billing-cycle direction from the same owner decision

- A missing outcome is allowed. Include the initial charge when its billing-cycle statement closes even if no outcome has been supplied. An outcome accepted after that cycle is assigned to a later cycle. Retain caller-reported occurrence time as evidence, but do not use it to backdate billing-period assignment. Existing outcome windows and authority checks still apply.
- Corrections are bounded by billing cycles. Before close, a usage correction is an append-only signed quantity delta linked to the accepted quantity; it does not replace or mutate the original record. After close, a change is a linked post-hoc adjustment in a later cycle and may be presented through a manually processed ad hoc receivable or payable statement. It must not revise a closed statement or overwrite accepted economic history. Exact correction authority, evidence, bounds, and adjustment-to-statement linkage are execution-plan details to settle before the affected code is implemented.
- Customer billing schedules are customer-level settings selected from a centrally governed set of supported terms, with as much valid customization as the system can safely support. The business fiscal reporting calendar is a separate, customizable organization-level setting for internal reporting. They do not control one another.
- Ledger acceptance time is the initial assignment clock for both the customer billing calendar and the organization fiscal reporting calendar. Other financial reports may later use separately retained dimensions.
- Closed standard statements are immutable. Manual ad hoc receivable/payable statements may be issued outside the standard cycle flow for linked post-hoc adjustments; they remain distinct from tax or legal invoices and do not execute payments.

### M5 execution defaults approved 2026-09-29

- Customer billing terms use a centrally validated finite interval grammar over days, weeks, months, and years, with IANA timezones, calendar arithmetic, half-open UTC periods, explicit month-end and DST rules, and effective-dated changes. Ordinary facts in both customer statements and organization-internal reports assign by ledger acceptance time; a pre-close correction may link to its original billing period while keeping its acceptance-time fiscal assignment.
- The organization fiscal calendar is separately versioned. Internal report runs pin a calendar version and accepted-ledger snapshot; declared 4-4-5-family week patterns include explicit 52/53-week behavior. A February 29 year-end anchor follows the existing Gregorian clamp: February 28 in non-leap years before weekday alignment.
- Recurrence is separate from customer billing periods. It creates no background charges; missed occurrences require explicit operator catch-up, automatic renewal is opt-in, cancellation takes effect at acceptance, the M2 acceptance-time agreement selection remains controlling, and proration defaults to none.
- Usage corrections are signed, append-only quantity deltas bounded by the original agreement, with original unit/rate basis and explicit correction authority. Post-close changes are linked adjustments in later periods; ad hoc statements identify included adjustments. Closed statements stay immutable.
- A period remains correction-eligible until the close record is durably committed, even after scheduled end. The initial customer term explicitly covers retained M4 history; activation/close refuse if any accepted billable entry is unassigned. Post-close adjustments default to the next standard statement; an operator may issue an ad hoc statement first, with exactly-once presentation.
- Cumulative activity is bucketed by customer period, agreement/version, mode/unit, conversion-rule version, currency and scale. Aggregate exactly, apply the pinned formula once, and round a finer-than-scale-18 result once at close using `nearest_ties_away`; retain the exact rational and booked atoms. Post-close cumulative deltas use the difference between the old and new booked totals, `B(q_new)-B(q_old)`, under the original rule, with no second rounding.
- USD scale-18 precision is retained through close and statements with no cent rounding. A standard statement shows only its own period net, without carrying forward prior unpaid balances; a negative net is represented as payable. Manual payment status, provider processing, and automatic expiry of accepted records/retry identities are excluded.

### M5 scope-control rule

The M5 milestone row, owner-approved defaults above, and frozen Package 3 acceptance contract define the M5 scope ceiling. Every implementation change must directly implement, verify, or protect one of those requirements. Required migrations, CLI paths, validation, and tests are in scope only to deliver that accepted behavior. Optional capabilities, generalizations, and conveniences without a direct requirement are deferred to a later milestone. “As much valid customization” means choices within the approved finite grammars; it does not authorize new billing modes or unbounded configuration. If delivery requires changing money, authority, assignment, identity retention, or the payment boundary, stop and bring the specific decision back to the owner before changing the canonical roadmap or frozen contract.

See the [M5 decision register](m5-decision-register.md) for normative defaults and stop conditions.

## Later policy gates

M4's versioned scale-18 usage setup, statement and export contracts are qualified for the named source-built profile and were released in v0.7.0. M5 adds append-only usage quantity deltas and post-close adjustments while preserving M4 records and precision. Multiple billable milestones or units per agreement, minimum charges, and explicit failed-work fees remain outside M5 unless separately approved. M5's owner defaults for billing terms, calendars, recurrence, late facts, correction bounds, statement precision/presentation, payment boundary and identity retention are recorded in the [M5 decision register](m5-decision-register.md) and qualified for the v0.8.0 source-built local profile. Closed statements are immutable; there is no reopen path in this scope. A separate tax/legal invoice artifact is not implied. These policies do not alter the completed M0–M4 source-built profiles.
