Make the value visible.

The previous version put too much visual weight on nested panels, repeated benefits, package descriptions, status labels and caveats. Its sample dollar amount explained almost none of the product’s useful complexity.

Changes made

  • One story: customer inputs become explainable, usable billing records.
  • A flow diagram replaces the illustrative receipt. Its labels remain readable HTML for agents and assistive technology.
  • One accent color and one primary action. Typography, alignment and space do the grouping.
  • Two operating choices. Managed delivery moves into details rather than competing as a third early decision.
  • Technical evidence and requirements stay available through progressive disclosure and a separate page.

Grounding

Applied principles from Impeccable and its design guidance: prioritize the visitor’s task, strengthen hierarchy, remove competing decoration, clarify language and distinguish persuasive pages from operating interfaces. This was a manual principles-based review; the Impeccable package and automated detector were not installed or run.

What would earn a strong selling-tool verdict?

Test whether an unfamiliar buyer can name the problem, identify what they must supply, describe the outputs and choose the next step. Measure task success and hesitation before claiming better conversion. Validate narrow-screen, keyboard and screen-reader use. No conversion lift, customer endorsement or usability score is claimed.

Checks and limits

Static resource/anchor, JSON and JavaScript checks are performed before publication. Selector behavior is checked with a synthetic DOM. Browser visual, mobile, keyboard and screen-reader verification remain uncompleted; responsive rules alone are not evidence that all layouts work.

Current product claims follow the pinned local-profile capability matrix. This proposal does not launch managed services or authorize product implementation.
