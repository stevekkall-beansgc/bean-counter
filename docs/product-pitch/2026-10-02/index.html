The hard billing problems you shouldn’t solve again.

01Link the money to the workEvents, charges & adjustments

The supported lifecycle connects successful work and usage to a charge, an optional authorized outcome adjustment, and later corrections. Each accepted record retains its identity and relationship to earlier work.

Why it matters: your application can explain which work created a charge and which later fact changed the amount, rather than treating every message as a new sale.

The boundary: generation → publication → acquisition with additional milestone charges is still a documented future story. Arbitrary multi-step chains, multiple billable milestones per agreement and linked supplier obligations are not a qualified end-to-end product.

02Protect against expensive billing mistakesIdentity, authority & precision
  • Duplicate billing: same-scope retries resolve to the saved result; conflicting reuse of an identity refuses.
  • Unauthorized changes: customer/source scope, permissions and expected revisions are checked before accepting operations.
  • Retroactive repricing: effective-dated agreement versions preserve the terms selected at first successful acceptance.
  • Lost fractional charges: the usage profile retains USD scale-18 precision. Cumulative conversion occurs once at close under the pinned rule.
  • Rewritten history: corrections append linked changes; closed statements never reopen.
  • False success: records commit before success is acknowledged. An uncertain result must be reconciled or retried with the original identity.

These are documented local-profile behaviors under the supported storage assumptions. They do not establish remote authentication, tamper-proof administration, universal exactly-once delivery or unlimited capacity.

03Report on the period that mattersCustomer cycles & fiscal calendars
  • Customer billing schedules: supported day/week/month/year intervals, IANA timezones, explicit month-end and daylight-saving rules.
  • Separate fiscal reporting: a versioned organization calendar, including supported 4-4-5-family patterns and 52/53-week behavior.
  • Reproducible close: immutable period statements and reports pinned to the relevant calendar version and ledger snapshot.
  • Late changes: post-close corrections become linked adjustments in later periods; an explicit ad hoc statement can present an adjustment.
  • Cumulative usage: bucket by period and agreement, aggregate precisely, then apply the agreed conversion.
  • Explicit recurrence: due occurrences and cancellation rules are retained. Recurrence does not silently run background charges.

Ordinary period assignment follows ledger acceptance time, not a caller-selected backdate. Statements preserve full precision and remain separate from payments and tax/legal invoices.
